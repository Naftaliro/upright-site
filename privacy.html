Upright Privacy Policy

Effective October 15, 2026

Upright is an alarm clock app for iPhone. This policy explains what happens to your information when you use it. The short version: Upright doesn't collect data about you. The only things that ever leave your phone are feedback you choose to send, alarm reports if you say yes to them, and Buddy Alerts if you use them.

What Upright stores

Your alarms (times, repeat days, labels, sounds, notes, and challenge settings), your app preferences, and your mornings history (when each alarm rang and when you finished its challenge, shown in the Mornings tab) are stored only on your iPhone. They are never sent to us or to anyone else. Deleting the app deletes them.

If you choose the Steps challenge, Upright counts your steps with your iPhone's motion sensor while the challenge is on screen, after asking for Motion & Fitness access. The count is used only to finish the challenge and isn't saved or sent anywhere.

Upright schedules your alarms through Apple's AlarmKit, the same system the Clock app uses, so iOS knows when to ring. That stays on your device as part of iOS.

What Upright does not do

  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • No analytics, tracking, or advertising.
  • No third-party code (SDKs) that collects data.
  • Upright only uses the internet for things you choose: sending feedback or alarm reports, and Buddy Alerts (below).

Wake-Up Buddy (optional)

If you choose a buddy, iOS's contact picker gives Upright only the name and phone number you pick, and they stay on your iPhone. Upright can open Messages with a short heads-up for your buddy, and after you solve a challenge, with "I'm up" filled in. You send these yourself, from your own number. Nothing about your buddy is sent to the developer.

Buddy Alerts (optional)

Buddy Alerts lets a friend's Upright tell them when you sleep through an alarm, and when you're up. It starts only when you invite a buddy or accept an invite. It uses a small server run by the developer on Cloudflare, which stores:

  • A random ID for your iPhone and a secret only your iPhone knows (the server keeps a one-way fingerprint of the secret, not the secret itself), and the first name you choose to show your buddies.
  • Your iPhone's notification address from Apple, so alerts can reach you.
  • Who is whose buddy, and invites that haven't been used yet.
  • If you've invited a buddy: the times of your next alarm rings (up to three days ahead) and when you finished each challenge, so the server can tell your buddies if you slept through or when you're up.

There's no account, email address or phone number. Rings are deleted after three days, unused invites after two days, and everything about an iPhone that hasn't used Buddy Alerts for six months. Leave Buddy Alerts in Settings deletes everything about you right away. As with any website, Cloudflare processes your IP address to deliver requests; the server doesn't record it. None of this is used for anything but Buddy Alerts.

Tips (optional)

Settings has optional tips. They're In-App Purchases handled entirely by Apple: Upright never sees your payment details, and a tip unlocks nothing.

After a tip you can add a name for the Special Thanks list in Settings. If you do, that name and which tip you left are sent to the developer, the same way as feedback, and the name may be shown to everyone in a later version. Tip amounts are never shown. Leave it empty to stay anonymous.

Feedback (optional)

Settings has a feedback form. It's anonymous by default and every question is optional. Nothing is sent until you tap Send. When you do:

  • Your answers go to a small relay run by the developer on Cloudflare, which emails them to the developer. The relay doesn't store them.
  • Unless you uncheck it, the message includes the app version, iOS version, and iPhone model (for example "iPhone18,4"), to help reproduce bugs. That doesn't identify you.
  • It only includes your name or contact details if you type them into the optional field.
  • If you check Attach Alarm Check report, it also includes that report (see below).
  • As with any website, Cloudflare processes your IP address to deliver the request. The relay doesn't record it or include it in the email.

If the relay can't be reached, the form offers to send through your Mail app instead. In that case the developer sees your email address, as with any email.

Feedback is used only to improve Upright.

Alarm reports (optional, your choice)

Settings → Alarm Check shows what Upright has scheduled and a log of recent alarm activity: alarms added or changed, when they rang, whether they were stopped or solved, when the app started or was updated, and crash details if the app crashed (provided by iOS through Apple's MetricKit). This log is kept only on your iPhone.

It leaves your phone only if you choose to send it:

  • Attach Alarm Check report in the feedback form sends it once, with that message.
  • Send reports automatically: after setup, Upright asks once whether to send reports, with Send Reports and Don't Send as equal choices. Nothing is sent until you choose Send Reports, or turn the switch on in Alarm Check. When on, Upright sends the report after alarm activity, at most once every few hours, so the developer can find bugs you might not notice.

A report contains your alarm times and labels, the activity log (including any crash details), the alarms permission setting, your time zone, and the app version, iOS version, and iPhone model. It goes through the same relay as feedback, which emails it to the developer and stores nothing. It contains no name, account, or advertising identifier, and isn't used for anything except fixing Upright. Turn the switch off at any time and nothing more is sent.

TestFlight

If you're testing a beta through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may share crash reports, usage information, and any feedback you send through TestFlight with the developer, as described in Apple's TestFlight terms. Upright itself doesn't add anything to that.

Children

Upright doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone, including children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date.
